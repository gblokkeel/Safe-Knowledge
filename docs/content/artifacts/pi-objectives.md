# PI Objectives

## Overview

**PI Objectives** are a summary of the business and technical goals that an Agile Team or ART intends to achieve in the upcoming Program Increment. They are created during PI Planning and represent the team's commitment to delivering specific outcomes during the PI.

PI Objectives are the primary mechanism by which:
- Teams communicate their planned contribution to Business Owners
- Business Owners score and prioritize team commitments
- Progress is tracked objectively throughout the PI
- ART predictability is measured at I&A

## Types of PI Objectives

### Committed Objectives
Goals the team is confident (80%+) they can achieve in the PI:
- Drive the core plan
- Business Owners score these during PI Planning
- Missing a committed objective is a significant event to be explained

### Stretch Objectives
Aspirational goals the team will pursue if capacity allows:
- Not scored by Business Owners during planning (but may be scored if achieved)
- Provide a buffer — if team finishes committed objectives early, they pull from stretch
- Reflect the team's ambition without over-committing

## Creating PI Objectives

PI Objectives are created by Agile Teams during PI Planning:

### Step 1: Review Features
Product Owner reviews the top features from the Program Backlog that the team will address this PI.

### Step 2: Create Draft Objectives
Teams translate features and stories into outcome-oriented objectives.

### Step 3: Map Stories to Objectives
Teams map their planned stories to specific PI Objectives, ensuring each objective has supporting work.

### Step 4: Assign Business Value
Business Owners score each committed objective (0–10) during the PI Planning review session.

## Writing Good PI Objectives

Good PI Objectives are:
- **Outcome-oriented** — Describe the business result, not the tasks
- **Specific** — Measurable enough to evaluate "done"
- **Realistic** — Achievable within the PI with reasonable confidence
- **Business-valuable** — Meaningful to the Business Owners

**Good example:**
> "Enable customers to complete checkout using saved payment methods, reducing checkout time by 40%"

**Poor example:**
> "Complete stories for payment feature" (activity-focused, not outcome-focused)

## Business Value Scoring

Business Owners score PI Objectives on a 0–10 scale:

| Score | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| 10 | Critical — must have; significant business impact if missed |
| 8–9 | Very high value |
| 6–7 | High value |
| 4–5 | Moderate value |
| 2–3 | Some value |
| 0–1 | Minimal or no value (should be reconsidered) |

If an objective scores 0, it should be removed from the PI plan.

## PI Predictability Measure

At the end of the PI (during I&A), Business Owners re-score each objective based on **actual achievement**:

```
PI Predictability = Σ(Actual Business Value Achieved) / Σ(Planned Business Value) × 100%
```

| Predictability | Interpretation |
|---------------|---------------|
| 80–100% | Good — ART is delivering reliably |
| 60–79% | Fair — some improvement needed |
| <60% | Poor — significant process issues |

Target: **80% or above**

Note: Stretch objectives, if achieved, are added to actual but not planned, which can push predictability above 100%.

## Team PI Objectives vs. ART PI Objectives

### Team PI Objectives
- Created by individual Agile Teams
- Reviewed and scored by Business Owners team-by-team
- Tracked at the team level throughout the PI

### ART PI Objectives (Optional)
- Created from the synthesis of all team objectives
- Represent the ART's overall PI commitments
- Used for communication to executives and portfolio management

## Using PI Objectives During Execution

PI Objectives are not just a planning artifact — they guide execution:
- ART Sync reviews progress against PI Objectives weekly
- Teams adjust sprint plans based on PI Objective status
- Risks that threaten PI Objectives are escalated quickly
- PO uses PI Objectives to prioritize within the sprint

## Common Challenges

| Challenge | Solution |
|----------|----------|
| Objectives are too task-focused | Practice writing outcome-based objectives |
| Teams commit to too many objectives | Better to have fewer committed, more stretch |
| Business Owners don't score | Educate BOs on their role; make it non-optional |
| No connection to Program Backlog | Ensure each objective maps to features |
| Objectives set once and forgotten | Review at every ART Sync |
