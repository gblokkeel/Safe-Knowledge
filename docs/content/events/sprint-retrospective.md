# Sprint Retrospective (Iteration Retrospective)

## Overview

The **Sprint Retrospective** (called **Iteration Retrospective** in SAFe) is the team's dedicated time to inspect their processes and identify improvements. It occurs at the end of each sprint, after the Sprint Review.

The Retrospective is one of the most powerful tools in Agile — when done well, it drives a culture of continuous improvement and psychological safety. When done poorly, it becomes a complaint session or is skipped entirely.

## Purpose

1. **Inspect** the team's processes, practices, and interactions
2. **Identify** what's working and what's not
3. **Create** a small number of specific improvement actions
4. **Commit** to those improvements in the next sprint
5. **Build** psychological safety and trust within the team

## Participants

- **All Team Members** — Required
- **Scrum Master** — Facilitates
- **Product Owner** — Participates as a team member

Note: External managers and stakeholders should generally NOT attend, as their presence can inhibit honest feedback.

## Duration

- **1-week sprint**: ~45 minutes
- **2-week sprint**: ~1–1.5 hours

## Common Retrospective Formats

### 1. Start / Stop / Continue
Teams identify:
- **Start**: Things we should begin doing
- **Stop**: Things we should stop doing
- **Continue**: Things that are working and should continue

Simple, easy to facilitate, good for new teams.

### 2. What Went Well / What Didn't / Improvements
Similar to Start/Stop/Continue but with a structured improvement section.

### 3. 4Ls: Liked / Learned / Lacked / Longed For
- **Liked**: What we enjoyed or that worked
- **Learned**: What we learned this sprint
- **Lacked**: What was missing
- **Longed For**: What we wish we had

### 4. Sailboat (Metaphor)
- **Wind** (propellers): What's helping us go faster?
- **Anchors**: What's slowing us down?
- **Rocks** (ahead): Risks we need to watch for
- **Island** (destination): Our goal

### 5. Mad / Sad / Glad
Emotionally focused; good for surfacing morale and team health issues.

### 6. Timeline Retrospective
Team plots events on a timeline; discuss what happened during the sprint chronologically.

## Retrospective Facilitation Guide

### Step 1: Set the Stage (5 min)
- Remind the team of the retrospective prime directive:
  > "Regardless of what we discover, we understand and truly believe that everyone did the best job they could, given what they knew at the time, their skills and abilities, the resources available, and the situation at hand."
- Create safety: remind that this is a blameless session

### Step 2: Gather Data (15–20 min)
- Each person writes observations on sticky notes
- Silent writing first (avoids groupthink)
- All voices captured, not just the loudest

### Step 3: Generate Insights (10–15 min)
- Cluster related items
- Identify themes
- Discuss: "Why is this happening?"

### Step 4: Decide What to Do (10–15 min)
- Vote on top improvement areas
- Select 1–3 specific improvement actions
- Assign owners and timelines

### Step 5: Close the Retrospective (5 min)
- Summarize commitments
- Quick team appreciation round
- SM closes the meeting

## Improvement Actions

Good improvement actions are:
- **Specific**: "We will set up automated linting in CI" not "improve code quality"
- **Assigned**: One person is accountable
- **Time-boxed**: Will be completed by end of next sprint
- **Tracked**: Written down and reviewed at the next retrospective

## Scaling: Sprint Retro → Inspect & Adapt

In SAFe, team retrospectives feed into the ART-level **Inspect & Adapt** workshop:
- Team-level improvements → team backlog
- Systemic (cross-team) issues → elevated to I&A problem-solving workshop
- ART-level improvements → ART improvement backlog (Sprint 1 stories next PI)

## Team Health Checks

Some teams use structured health check tools:
- **Spotify Squad Health Check** — Visual heatmap of team health
- **Team Radar** — Score multiple dimensions of team health
- **NPS for team** — Simple happiness/engagement measure

Regular health checks make invisible team dynamics visible and trackable.

## Common Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Impact |
|-------------|--------|
| **Retrospective skipped** — "No time" | Problems compound; team learns they don't matter |
| **Manager in the room** | Team self-censors; real issues not surfaced |
| **No action items** | Teams lose faith that retros are worthwhile |
| **Same action items every sprint** | Root causes not addressed |
| **SM does all the talking** | Team not engaged; SM's retrospective, not the team's |
| **Only negative focus** | Demoralizing; misses the positives worth amplifying |
