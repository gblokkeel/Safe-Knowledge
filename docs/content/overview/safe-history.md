# History of SAFe

## Origins

The Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe) was created by **Dean Leffingwell** and first publicly released in 2011. Leffingwell, a software engineering consultant and author of *Agile Software Requirements* (2011), developed SAFe based on his direct experience helping large enterprises adopt Agile practices.

## Timeline

### Pre-SAFe (2000s)
- Leffingwell began working with large enterprises attempting to scale Agile
- Observed that team-level Agile practices broke down at the program and portfolio levels
- Developed the concept of the "Agile Release Train" as a key scaling mechanism
- Published *Scaling Software Agility* (2007) documenting early patterns

### SAFe 1.0 (2011)
- First public release of the Scaled Agile Framework
- Introduced the Big Picture concept
- Focused primarily on Team and Program levels
- Introduced Program Increment and PI Planning

### SAFe 2.0 (2012)
- Added Portfolio Level
- Introduced Value Streams and Epic ownership
- Strengthened portfolio management guidance

### SAFe 3.0 (2013–2014)
- Major revision adding Systems Thinking
- Improved DevOps integration
- Expanded the Lean-Agile Mindset content
- Added more prescriptive guidance on roles and events

### SAFe 4.0 (2016)
- Introduced "Program" and "Value Stream" (later renamed "Large Solution") levels
- Added Lean UX and DevOps pipeline
- Released SAFe for Lean Enterprises
- Significant expansion of guidance around customer centricity

### SAFe 4.5 (2017)
- Refined Large Solution Level
- Added deeper guidance on Continuous Delivery Pipeline
- Improved portfolio Kanban guidance

### SAFe 4.6 (2018)
- Released alongside updated certifications
- Improved accessibility and organization of the online knowledge base
- Added more guidance on Lean Budgets

### SAFe 5.0 (2020)
- Landmark release introducing **Business Agility**
- Introduced 7 Core Competencies
- Added Organizational Agility and Continuous Learning Culture competencies
- Strengthened OKR guidance at the portfolio level
- Renamed roles (e.g., "Value Stream Engineer" → "Solution Train Engineer")
- Updated Core Values to include "Flow"

### SAFe 5.1 (2021)
- Clarifications and improvements to SAFe 5.0
- Updated certification content
- Better guidance on remote and hybrid PI Planning

### SAFe 6.0 (2023)
- Focused on **Flow** as a central concept
- Introduced **Eight Flow Accelerators**
- Strengthened guidance on OKRs at all levels
- Simplified and streamlined guidance in several areas
- Updated competency model
- Improved guidance on Agile Teams (including Kanban teams at scale)

## Founding Company: Scaled Agile, Inc.

**Scaled Agile, Inc.** was founded to steward the SAFe framework and build the ecosystem around it:
- Maintains the online knowledge base at framework.scaledagile.com
- Runs the SAFe training and certification program
- Manages the SAFe Partner ecosystem (training providers and consultants)
- Publishes case studies and research

## SAFe Adoption

SAFe has grown to become the **most widely adopted scaling framework** for Agile in enterprises:
- Used by over 20,000 organizations worldwide
- Adopted across industries: technology, banking, healthcare, defense, government, retail
- Hundreds of certified training partners globally
- Multiple certification levels from SA (SAFe Agilist) to SPC (SAFe Program Consultant)

## Key Certifications

| Certification | Level |
|--------------|-------|
| SAFe Agilist (SA) | Introductory — for leaders and managers |
| SAFe Practitioner (SP) | For all ART team members |
| SAFe Scrum Master (SSM) | For Scrum Masters |
| SAFe Product Owner/Product Manager (POPM) | For POs and PMs |
| SAFe Release Train Engineer (RTE) | For RTEs |
| SAFe Lean Portfolio Manager (LPM) | For portfolio managers |
| SAFe Program Consultant (SPC) | Advanced — for coaches and trainers |
| SAFe Program Consultant Trainer (SPCT) | For licensed SAFe trainers |

## Influence and Criticism

### Positive Impact
- Brought structure to enterprise Agile scaling
- Provided shared vocabulary for large organizations
- Enabled many enterprises to dramatically improve delivery speed and quality
- Created a large community of practitioners

### Common Criticisms
- Perceived as "too prescriptive" by some Agile purists
- Heavy upfront configuration can slow initial adoption
- Risk of creating "Agile in name only" if cultural change doesn't follow
- Certification model commercializes what some consider should be freely shared

### Response
Scaled Agile responds to these criticisms through ongoing framework simplification (especially in SAFe 6.0) and by emphasizing that SAFe is a guide, not a prescription.
