# Epic Owner

## Overview

The **Epic Owner** is responsible for coordinating the portfolio Epics through the Portfolio Kanban and ensuring they are analyzed and properly broken down into Features and Capabilities that can be executed by ARTs.

Epic Owners are typically Product Managers, Business Analysts, Enterprise Architects, or other product/technical leaders who have the breadth and depth to manage large, cross-cutting investments.

## Responsibilities

### Champion the Epic
- Define and maintain the Epic's **Lean Business Case**
- Advocate for the Epic through the Portfolio Kanban
- Communicate the Epic's value and urgency to stakeholders

### Analyze and Decompose
- Conduct the Lean Business Case analysis
- Break down the Epic into **Features** (business Epics) or **Capabilities** (large solution Epics)
- Ensure Features are ready for Program Backlog prioritization

### Coordinate Execution
- Coordinate with Product Managers across ARTs
- Track Epic-level progress across multiple ARTs
- Identify and manage cross-ART dependencies
- Report Epic status to LPM

### Manage the MVP
- Define the **Minimum Viable Product (MVP)** for the Epic
- Ensure the MVP hypothesis is testable
- Evaluate MVP results to determine: continue, pivot, or stop

## Epic Types

| Epic Type | Description | Owner |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| **Business Epic** | New customer-facing capabilities | Business Epic Owner |
| **Enabler Epic** | Infrastructure and architecture investments | Technical Epic Owner (often Enterprise Architect) |

## The Lean Business Case

Every Epic requires a Lean Business Case before implementation approval. It includes:

### 1. Epic Hypothesis Statement
```
For [customers]
Who [need or opportunity]
The [solution]
Is a [category]
That [benefit/value]
Unlike [competitor or current solution]
Our solution [key differentiator]
```

### 2. Leading Indicators
- How will we know if the Epic is succeeding?
- What metrics will we track?
- What does success look like in 3, 6, 12 months?

### 3. Non-Functional Requirements (NFRs)
- Performance, security, compliance requirements
- Scalability and operational considerations

### 4. In-Scope / Out-of-Scope
- What is included in this Epic?
- What is explicitly excluded?

### 5. Analysis Summary
- Research findings
- Technical feasibility assessment
- Risk identification

### 6. Go/No-Go Recommendation
- Should the Epic be approved for the Portfolio Backlog?
- What are the key risks and mitigations?

## Epic Owner in the Portfolio Kanban

| Kanban Stage | Epic Owner Activity |
|-------------|-------------------|
| **Funnel** | Capture the idea; initial description |
| **Review** | LPM reviews; Epic Owner provides context |
| **Analyzing** | Develop the Lean Business Case |
| **Portfolio Backlog** | Epic approved; coordinate with PM for Feature breakdown |
| **Implementing** | Track progress; manage cross-ART coordination |
| **Done** | Measure outcomes; close the Lean Business Case |

## Skills Required

| Skill | Why It Matters |
|-------|---------------|
| **Business Acumen** | Understand the economic value of the Epic |
| **Technical Understanding** | Assess feasibility and dependencies |
| **Communication** | Present the case to LPM; coordinate across ARTs |
| **Analytical Thinking** | Develop the Lean Business Case rigorously |
| **Stakeholder Management** | Navigate competing interests |

## Common Challenges

- **Large Epic syndrome** — Epics that are too large to ever finish; Epic Owner must aggressively decompose
- **No single Epic Owner** — Epics without a clear owner drift and lose momentum
- **Over-analysis** — Lean Business Case becomes a waterfall requirements document
- **Ownership without authority** — Epic Owner coordinates but doesn't control ART backlogs
