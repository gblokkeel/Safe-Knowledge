# Lean-Agile Leadership

## Overview

**Lean-Agile Leadership** is one of SAFe's 7 Core Competencies and the foundation of Business Agility. Lean-Agile leaders create and sustain the culture and environment needed for high-performing Agile teams and ARTs to succeed.

The key insight: **A SAFe transformation is primarily a leadership transformation.** No amount of process change will succeed if leadership continues to operate in a command-and-control mindset.

## The Leadership Shift

| Traditional Leadership | Lean-Agile Leadership |
|-----------------------|----------------------|
| Command and control | Servant leadership |
| Manage people | Design and improve systems |
| Analyze and plan upfront | Learn by doing; embrace uncertainty |
| Protect from bad news | Encourage transparency; welcome bad news early |
| Optimize teams individually | Optimize flow across the system |
| Individual performance management | Team and system performance |
| Annual strategy | Continuous portfolio management |
| Decisions flow down | Decisions pushed to competent levels |
| Success = on-time, on-budget | Success = value delivered to customers |

## Three Core Dimensions of Lean-Agile Leadership

### 1. Mindset and Principles

Leaders must internalize and role-model the **Lean-Agile Mindset**:
- **Growth mindset** — Embrace learning from failure
- **Lean thinking** — Continuously seek to eliminate waste
- **Agile values** — Individuals over process; responding to change
- **SAFe Principles** — Understand and apply the 10 principles in every decision

Leaders who merely comply with SAFe ceremonies while maintaining old thinking will undermine the transformation.

### 2. Leading by Example

Leaders demonstrate Lean-Agile behaviors through personal conduct:
- **Attend PI Planning** — Not just kick-off speeches; stay engaged throughout
- **Visit teams** — Gemba walks ("go to where the work is done")
- **Retrospect publicly** — Share their own mistakes and learnings
- **Ask questions rather than give answers** — "What do you think?" over "Here's what you should do"
- **Celebrate experimentation** — Recognize teams that try new things, not just teams that succeed

### 3. Leading Change

Leaders are the primary agents of Lean-Agile transformation:
- Build the **Urgency for Change** — Articulate why transformation is necessary
- Create the **Vision for Change** — What will we look like when we succeed?
- Develop the **Plan for Change** — LACE, training, coaching investment
- **Clear the path** — Remove organizational impediments to Agile adoption
- **Sustain momentum** — Transformations take 2–5 years; leaders must sustain commitment

## SAFe Leadership Behaviors

Based on Kotter's 8-step change model and adapted for SAFe:

### Lean-Agile Leaders:

**Think Systemically**
- Understand the organization as a system
- Avoid local optimization
- See and address root causes, not symptoms

**Create Organizational Agility**
- Design organizations that can respond to change
- Remove bureaucratic impediments to flow
- Enable rapid redeployment of resources to high-value work

**Foster Learning and Innovation**
- Create time and space for experimentation (IP Iteration)
- Celebrate learning from failure
- Fund Communities of Practice
- Support continuous skill development

**Nurture Psychological Safety**
- Create an environment where people feel safe to speak up
- Never punish bad news
- Encourage healthy debate and disagreement
- Protect people from retribution for mistakes

**Unlock Intrinsic Motivation**
- Provide autonomy, mastery, and purpose (Pink's Drive)
- Connect people's work to meaningful outcomes
- Invest in people's growth

## The Lean-Agile Leader's Role in Transformations

### 3-Phase Leadership Journey

**Phase 1: Learn**
- Attend a SAFe Leading SAFe or SPC training
- Observe high-performing Agile teams
- Read: Toyota Kata, Drive, The Fifth Discipline

**Phase 2: Model**
- Change your own behaviors first
- Be visible in your Agile commitment
- Show vulnerability — demonstrate growth mindset

**Phase 3: Coach**
- Develop other leaders
- Establish the LACE (Lean-Agile Center of Excellence)
- Create a community of Lean-Agile leaders

## The LACE: Lean-Agile Center of Excellence

The **LACE** is a small team of Lean-Agile change agents who drive the SAFe transformation:
- Typically 2–10 people depending on organization size
- Includes: RTEs, coaches, champions, HR, L&D
- Reports to senior leadership
- Responsibilities: Training, coaching, measuring, improving the transformation

## Common Leadership Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Impact |
|-------------|--------|
| **"SAFe is for the teams, not me"** | Leaders undermine the culture they ask teams to adopt |
| **Skipping PI Planning** | Sends signal that planning doesn't matter; misses alignment |
| **Assigning individual tasks** | Destroys self-organization and team ownership |
| **Punishing bad news** | Creates a culture of hiding problems |
| **Reverting under pressure** | "In a crisis, we go back to waterfall" — never builds real Agile capability |
| **Celebrating only successes** | Experiments become career risks; innovation dies |

## Leadership and Continuous Improvement

Every leadership team should have its own retrospective cadence:
- What are we doing that's enabling the transformation?
- What are we doing that's undermining it?
- What do we need to start/stop/continue?
