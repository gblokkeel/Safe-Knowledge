# PI Planning

## Overview

**PI Planning** is the heartbeat of the Agile Release Train. It is a face-to-face (or virtual) event that aligns all teams on the ART to a shared mission and vision and sets the PI Objectives for the upcoming Program Increment.

PI Planning is typically held every 8–12 weeks (the duration of a PI), involves the entire ART (50–125 people), and runs for two days.

> *"If you are not doing PI Planning, you are not doing SAFe."* — Dean Leffingwell

## Purpose

PI Planning serves several critical purposes:
1. **Align all teams** to the same mission, vision, and top priorities
2. **Identify and plan for dependencies** between teams
3. **Create team PI Objectives** — commitments for the PI
4. **Surface and manage risks**
5. **Build social bonds** across teams that will work together for the next quarter

## Participants

| Role | Attendance |
|------|------------|
| **Entire ART** (all teams, POs, SMs) | Required |
| **Release Train Engineer** | Required — facilitates the event |
| **Product Manager** | Required — presents Vision and Features |
| **System Architect** | Required — presents Architectural Vision |
| **Business Owners** | Required — score PI Objectives |
| **Customers** | Highly encouraged — present real needs |
| **Executive Sponsor** | Opening remarks; demonstrates leadership support |

## PI Planning Agenda (2 Days)

### Day 1

**Morning:**
- Executive/Business context (Business Owners or executive)
- Product Management Vision (top 10 features for the PI)
- Architectural Vision and development practices
- Planning context and ground rules (RTE)

**Afternoon:**
- Team breakout session 1
  - Teams estimate capacity for each sprint of the PI
  - Teams draft sprint-by-sprint plans for each feature
  - Teams identify inter-team dependencies
  - Teams create initial PI Objectives
  - Teams identify risks

**Evening (optional):**
- Teams continue planning informally
- Social activities to build relationships

### Day 2

**Morning:**
- Team breakout session 2 (continue planning, refine objectives)
- Business Owner review of draft PI Objectives

**Afternoon:**
- Risk review (ROAM session)
- Final PI Objective presentations by each team
- Business Owner scoring of PI Objectives
- PI Planning retrospective
- Planning confidence vote ("Fist of Five")
- PI go/no-go decision

## PI Objectives

PI Planning produces **PI Objectives** — SMART business and technical goals for the PI:

- **Committed Objectives** — High confidence (70–80%+ probability)
- **Stretch Objectives** — Aspirational goals the team will pursue if capacity allows

Teams present their objectives to Business Owners who:
1. Validate business alignment
2. Score each objective (0–10 business value)

## The Program Board

During PI Planning, teams create the **Program Board** — a visual display showing:
- Features planned for each sprint across all teams
- Dependencies between teams (colored strings on physical boards)
- Key milestones and external dependencies

The Program Board enables the entire ART to see the integrated plan and identify coordination problems.

## Risk Management: ROAM

At the end of PI Planning, risks are **ROAM**ed:

| Letter | Action | Meaning |
|--------|--------|---------|
| **R** | Resolved | The risk has been mitigated or removed |
| **O** | Owned | A specific person/team owns the risk and will manage it |
| **A** | Accepted | The risk is known and accepted; no action taken |
| **M** | Mitigated | A plan exists to reduce the risk's probability or impact |

## Planning Confidence Vote

After the final plan is presented, the RTE facilitates a "Fist of Five" vote:
- Each person holds up 1–5 fingers:
  - **5** = "I am highly confident we can achieve this plan"
  - **3** = "I am somewhat confident; I have some concerns"
  - **1** = "I am not confident; the plan has significant issues"

If the average is below 3, the team identifies the main impediments and adjusts the plan.

## Virtual PI Planning

For distributed teams, PI Planning can be run virtually with modifications:
- Use collaborative tools (Miro, Confluence, MURAL) for the Program Board
- Schedule in overlapping time zones
- Break planning into more focused sessions
- Increase one-on-one coordination beforehand
- Over-invest in social connection activities

## Benefits of PI Planning

| Benefit | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Alignment** | 100% of the ART starts with shared understanding |
| **Dependency identification** | Problems found in planning, not execution |
| **Commitment** | Teams commit to outcomes they defined |
| **Risk reduction** | Risks surfaced early when mitigation is cheap |
| **Social bonding** | Face-to-face (or video) time builds trust |
| **Predictability** | Quarterly commitments enable business planning |

## Common Challenges and Solutions

| Challenge | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| Too many people for one room | Split into smaller rooms with synchronized sessions |
| Business Owners can't attend | Escalate — this is a senior leadership responsibility |
| Teams don't know enough to plan | Refine features and enablers before PI Planning (Backlog Grooming) |
| Plans fall apart immediately | Use IP Iteration as a buffer; plans are not commitments to details |
| Virtual engagement drops | Smaller time blocks; more facilitators; better collaboration tools |
