# Principle #9 – Decentralize Decision-Making

## Statement

> *"Decentralize decision-making"*

In a scaled Agile enterprise, no single person or small group can make all the decisions needed to deliver value continuously. Centralizing decisions creates bottlenecks, slows delivery, and fails to leverage the knowledge of people closest to the work. SAFe advocates decentralizing decision-making to the **lowest competent level** to maximize speed and engagement.

## The Case for Decentralization

In traditional command-and-control organizations:
- Decisions flow up to managers, then back down as instructions
- Managers are often furthest from the actual work
- Escalation takes time — often the decision arrives too late
- People are disempowered and disengaged

The cost of centralized decision-making:
- **Speed** — Waiting for decisions creates wait states (waste)
- **Quality** — Decisions made without direct knowledge of the situation
- **Engagement** — People feel like cogs, not contributors
- **Scalability** — Management bottlenecks as the organization grows

## What Gets Decentralized?

Not all decisions should be decentralized. SAFe provides a framework for thinking about this:

### Decentralize Decisions That Are:
- Frequent and time-sensitive
- Require local context (knowledge closest to the problem)
- Reversible (can be corrected if wrong)
- In the team's area of expertise
- Low in economic impact if wrong

### Centralize Decisions That Are:
- Rare but significant
- Require broad context across multiple teams/domains
- Irreversible or very costly to reverse
- Have large economic consequences
- Affect the entire organization or system architecture

## Empowering Teams

Decentralization requires that teams have:

### 1. Clear Mission and Boundaries
Teams can only make good decisions if they understand:
- The strategic direction ("what we're trying to achieve")
- The constraints they operate within
- The values and principles that guide decisions

Without this, decentralized decisions become random rather than aligned.

### 2. Competence
Decentralized decision-making is only safe when the people making decisions are competent to do so. This requires:
- Investment in training and skill development
- Coaching and mentorship
- Communities of Practice to spread knowledge

### 3. Access to Information
Good decisions require good information:
- Teams need visibility into program priorities
- Teams need architecture guidance
- Teams need customer and market feedback
- Backlogs and plans should be transparent

### 4. Accountability
With authority comes accountability:
- Teams own their commitments
- Teams demonstrate their work at Sprint Reviews and System Demos
- Retrospectives drive continuous improvement

## SAFe Mechanisms for Decentralization

| Mechanism | How It Decentralizes |
|----------|---------------------|
| **Self-organizing teams** | Teams determine how to accomplish sprint goals |
| **Product Owner authority** | PO has real authority to accept/reject stories |
| **Team Backlog ownership** | Teams manage their own backlog |
| **Iteration Goals** | Teams commit to their own sprint goals |
| **Communities of Practice** | Technical decisions made by community consensus |
| **Decentralized architecture** | Architectural decisions influenced by all levels |
| **PI Planning** | Teams plan their own PI Objectives |

## Lean Decision-Making Framework

SAFe recommends this decision-making framework:

1. **Decide as late as responsibly possible** — More information available; options preserved
2. **Decide as quickly as necessary** — Don't let caution become paralysis
3. **Decide at the right level** — Lowest competent level
4. **Make decisions visible** — Transparent, auditable decisions
5. **Make decisions reversible** — Design systems to allow course correction

## The Role of Intent-Based Leadership

SAFe draws on L. David Marquet's "Intent-Based Leadership" concept:
- Instead of "Here's what you need to do," leaders say "Here's what we're trying to achieve"
- Instead of asking for permission, teams state their intent
- Leaders respond to intent with "I concur" or provide redirection

This shifts the cognitive work from management to the people closest to the problem.

## Common Anti-Patterns

- **Approval chains** — Features requiring multiple sign-offs that slow delivery
- **Manager task assignment** — Managers directing what each person works on each day
- **Architecture dictated top-down** — No team input into technical decisions
- **Escalation culture** — Teams escalate decisions they are competent to make
- **Hero culture** — One person (the hero) makes all the key technical decisions
